Traditional and cultural practice

What these are. The practices below belong to the cultural tradition this chart sits inside. They have no scientifically established efficacy for changing events. They are offered as discipline, family observance and peace of mind — not as treatment, and not as a substitute for professional advice.

Why they are listed at all. Only where a mechanism has been repeatedly validated should a corresponding remedy be investigated. Since the event-level validation returned NO-GO, no mechanism is repeatedly validated, so these are traditional observances of general applicability rather than targeted prescriptions.

Explicitly not recommended

Deliberate omissions
  • No gemstones. No stone has been recommended. None is required, and a costly prescription is not a finding.
  • No tantric or ritual practice. Nothing dangerous, coercive or expensive is suggested, and nothing here should displace medical or psychological care.
  • No remedy invented for a difficult period. A difficult year is not itself evidence that a remedy is indicated.
  • No effort beyond ordinary observance. Traditional practice that demands significant time or money is out of proportion to what is actually known about it.
Safety
No practice here is medical or financial advice. No health condition is treated. No investment is recommended. For a real health or financial concern, use a qualified clinician or adviser.
