4. **It also contains the P1A Ashtakavarga chapter** (Ch. 66) with the Rekha totals.

**Rejected alternatives:** Vol. I of the same translation — zero Kuja hits. Girdhari Lal 1944 — Kuja absent and Ch. 66 prose absent from its OCR. Girish Chatur/Sharma and Suresh Chandra Mishra — not yet inspected.

## 5. Exact relevant passages

### 5.1 Formation — Ch. 80, verse 47

> **47.** The woman born becomes a widow, if Mars be in the 12th, 4th, 7th or 8th from the Ascendant unaspected by or associated with any benefic.

### 5.2 Cancellation — Ch. 80, verses 48–49

> **48-49.** The yoga which causes the woman to become a widow also causes a male native to become a widower. If the man and woman possessing this yoga join in wedlock, the yoga ceases to have any effect.

Accompanying Devanagari as OCR'd (corrupt — not relied upon):
> `qfroftm % nfiqpat ynfrn i rvt *sranft<iw Wn Otttottt iiy&ii`

### 5.3 Translator's identifying note — Ch. 80, Notes after v.49

> **Notes:** Actually the man or woman having in their nativity Mars in the houses mentioned in verse 47 are called Mangali or Mangalik. It is believed that if a Manglik girl marries a Mangalik boy, the evil effects of being Manglik cease to exist.

Note carefully: the **term** "Manglik" is the *translator's/expositor's* explanatory note. The verse itself does not use the word. The term is not thereby a separate classical rule.

## 7. Translation issues

1. **OCR corruption of the Sanskrit.** Both Devanagari lines above are degraded and *have not* been transcribed into rules. Only the English prose was relied on. No Sanskrit-text-backed specification is claimed.
2. **House-count conflict with modern sources.** The inspected English gives **four** houses (12th, 4th, 7th, 8th). The modern rule gives **six** (adding 1st and 2nd). At least one lead (`askmahdeva.com`) quotes a Devanagari shloka beginning `लग्ने व्यये सुखे च सप्तमे रन्ध्रे` — which **includes the 1st house**, contradicting the inspected English. **This conflict is unresolved and cannot be settled from this text layer.**
3. **Scope.** v.47 addresses **a woman's horoscope** and predicts **her widowhood**. It is not a general native-wide "Manglik" test.
4. **Sex asymmetry.** vv. 48–49 extend the yoga to a male becoming widower, but the mutual-cancellation sentence presupposes two people *each* holding the yoga.
5. **Verse-number variance.** External sources variously cite "Ch. 80", "Ch. 81", "vv. 47–48". Edition-specific citation is mandatory.
6. **"Traditional" label withheld.** Nothing here is labelled classical beyond "printed in this edition".

## 12. P1A — Ashtakavarga, same volume

Ch. 66 states contributor rules in the mandated form (target → contributor → relative houses), with Rekhaprada verse ranges for all eight including the Ascendant: Sun ~20–22; Moon 46–48; **Mercury 51–52**; Jupiter 53–55; Venus 56–58; Saturn 59–60; Mars 49–50; Ascendant 65–68.

**The Rekha totals printed in Ch. 66 match the P1A gate exactly:** Sun 48, Moon 49, Mars 39, **Mercury 54**, Jupiter 56, Venus 52, Saturn 39 — sum **337**. The previously-unsourced **Mercury 54 is now sourced as a published total in this chapter.**

**But the contributor prose could not be transcribed reliably.** Summing the transcribed rows gives 277 vs 337, short on *every* planet. The cause is visible: the OCR silently drops contributor names inside these passages (Venus reads *"6th : M«C"y a " Mars"*, *"a in e 11th"*; Jupiter reads *"all except the S in-the 9th"* where a planet list is required). **Encoding these would be transcription guessing.**

Therefore: **`AV_TABLES` unchanged; Mercury/Jupiter/Venus remain excluded; SAV remains 337 as a gate figure only, unreported.** Resolution requires a clean text layer or the page images.

**Independence.** The Kuja finding (Ch. 80) and the Ashtakavarga finding (Ch. 66) come from one *volume* but are separate passages, separate verse ranges and separate specifications. Each must carry its own `exact_text`, `source_ref`, tests and implementation specification. Neither inherits the other's interpretive status.
